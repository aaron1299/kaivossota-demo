KAIVOSSOTA

A one against one mining duel decided by route planning, timing and nerve. Two machines dig from opposite ends of the same mine toward the ore in the middle. Ore only becomes money once it is carried back to your own shaft, and the two machines can destroy each other on the way.

Player versus player, house takes commission No random source in the simulation Replayable and checksummed rounds Runs on a phone browser, no engine EN / FI
live build, playable

Practice mode runs against a rule-based bot and needs no server. Stake matches connect to the match server, which runs the only authoritative simulation. Served from kaivossota.onrender.com the stake match is live and you can play one against a colleague; the copy on GitHub Pages is static and offers practice only.

Why an operator would want this

Player versus player with a commission means the house is never a party to the match and carries no risk on the outcome. The margin is the commission on settled pots, and a draw refunds both stakes with no commission taken.

Because the simulation contains no random source, the usual RNG certification question changes shape. There is no generator to evaluate statistically. What has to be shown instead is that a recorded match replays to the same result, and that check runs from a command line in seconds against the stored input log.

Losing is not always zero. Ore banked at your own shaft is safe, so a player who mined well but lost the final fight still takes a share of the pot. That keeps a skill game playable for the losing side without the house paying for it.

Round economics

Stake levels1.00, 5.00 and 20.00 credits, set by the match server, all amounts handled as integer cents
PotTwo equal stakes. Both players put in the same amount
Commission8.0 % of the pot by default, expressed in parts per million and rounded half up to the nearest cent
WinnerAt least 60 % of the pot after commission
LoserThe remainder in proportion to the ore they banked. With nothing banked, nothing paid
DrawBoth stakes refunded in full, no commission
DisconnectThe player who left loses the match, and still receives their banked share. Commission is charged normally
DesyncIf a client state checksum differs from the server, the match is stopped as a draw and both stakes are refunded

Technical facts

Engine and deliveryNo game engine. Written directly against the browser canvas, delivered as static files and embedded as an iframe
HeadersNo special headers required, no service worker, no WebAssembly, no WebGL
Download size76 kB of code plus about 1.5 MB of images
OrientationPortrait 9:16, touch and mouse, mobile first
LanguagesEnglish and Finnish, switchable at runtime by the operator
Match length2 min 40 s, plus 30 s overtime if the ore counts are level. The mine holds more ore than either player can mine in that time, so the clock decides the match and the win comes from efficiency
Simulation30 ticks per second, integer arithmetic only, identical code on client and server
NetcodeLockstep on a fixed 200 ms input delay, identical for both players
Anti-cheatServer validates every move and owns the team assignment, clients send state checksums every 2 seconds
Records per roundInput log, final checksum, settlement row with stake, pot, commission and banked ore
CombatA strike only lands the way the machine is facing. Head to head both sides lose hull, a hit to the side or the back does one and a half times the damage and costs the attacker nothing, and two machines cannot share a square, so a narrow tunnel can be blocked. Position, not reflex, decides a fight
MapFixed and mirrored. Both players face the same rock hardness and the same distance to the ore, and nothing is hidden from either side
AssetsMachines and mine tiles are 3D renders produced by our own Blender scripts in this repository. All sound is synthesised at runtime. The only third-party files are CC0 surface textures, which carry no attribution requirement
Fairness proofThe replay viewer recomputes the match checksum in the browser and compares it against the recording, and the same check runs from the command line

Honest status

This is a working prototype, not a certified product. Verified by running: the deterministic simulation, replay reproduction, mirror symmetry of the map, combat and cargo rules, settlement to the cent, and a full networked match between two clients that stayed in the server state for the whole match.

Not done: authentication against an operator session, a real wallet connection, load testing, balance tuning with live players, a security audit and any test-house certification. Balances shown in this build are demo credits and no money moves anywhere.

Responsible gaming controls belong to the operator account layer. The game exposes set_reality_check so the operator can drive its own reminder policy, and ships no nagging of its own.
